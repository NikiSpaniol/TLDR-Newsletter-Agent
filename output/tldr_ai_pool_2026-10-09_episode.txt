Now, a reality check courtesy of Tony Fadell — the guy who designed the iPod and iPhone. He's been picking apart why the first wave of AI gadgets, the Rabbit R1, the Humane pin, the Limitless pendant, all flopped. His argument: they solved no real problem, because most people have never had a personal assistant and don't actually know what they'd do with one. Even he, by his own admission, took years to trust an assistant with sensitive data. His view is that only companies with existing hardware, existing trust, and existing scale — Apple, basically — can credibly pull this off, which is also why Meta and OpenAI are building new devices: they don't have the sensors already sitting in your pocket. For anyone doing customer discovery on an AI product, this is a useful cautionary tale to keep in the back pocket — technical capability is not the same as validated demand, and it's worth stress-testing any proposed AI product against a simple question: did anyone actually ask for this, or did we just build it because we could.
