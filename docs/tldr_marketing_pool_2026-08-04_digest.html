A geoSurge research report found that AI models search for brands they already remember from training at 3.2 times the rate of brands outside their memory, analyzing nearly 4,000 model responses across nine industries (Travel, Automotive, Finance, Business Software, Education, Food & Restaurants, Luxury, Fitness & Wellness, and Fashion) with 66 U.S. buyer questions. Brands in the model's top-10 memory were searched 55.7% of the time versus 17.4% for non-remembered brands, and this gap held across all nine industries with not-remembered search rates running 9–23% and remembered rates 41–82%. The effect is most pronounced in "memory-led" categories like Automotive (82% of brand-specific searches name top-5 remembered brands) and Finance (77%), while looser in categories like Fitness & Wellness (50%). The research demonstrates that model memory is formed at training time through sustained category authority—analyst coverage, partnerships, and consistent brand-category association—rather than optimized at query time through on-page content alone.

Strategic hookBrands should prioritize building sustained category authority and analyst coverage to establish presence in AI model training memory, particularly in memory-led categories where pre-search recall determines inclusion in the consideration set.

FROM TOBACCO TO TESLA, HOW SUBVERTISING HAS HELD UP A MIRROR TO SOME OF THE BIGGEST BRANDS (6 MINUTE READ)

hook

Subvertising—the practice of hijacking or spoofing brand advertisements to critique them—has evolved from spontaneous billboard graffiti in the 1970s into sophisticated coordinated campaigns that can significantly damage brand perception.

Subvertising—the practice of hijacking or spoofing brand advertisements to critique them—has evolved from spontaneous billboard graffiti in the 1970s into sophisticated coordinated campaigns that can significantly damage brand perception. Early examples include the Billboard Liberation Front (founded 1977) and BUGA UP, which targeted tobacco advertising in Australia through modified billboards; BUGA UP's efforts may have contributed to Australia banning billboard tobacco ads in 1993. The practice professionalized in the 1990s with Canada-based Adbusters creating polished "anti-adverts" that highlighted issues like eating disorders in fashion advertising, and later evolved through groups like Brandalism (founded 2012) coordinating global campaigns against fossil fuel companies and airlines. Recent targets include Tesla and Meta, with the group Everyone Hates Elon using lenticular overlays and provocative imagery to undermine brand messaging. Research from a 2023 Psychology and Marketing study found that targeted subvertising negatively affects brand perception, word-of-mouth, and purchase intention. The article concludes that brands can protect themselves by maintaining genuine values aligned with their messaging and product practices, as subvertising gains traction precisely when it exposes real ethical problems like greenwashing or hypocrisy.

Strategic hookUnderstanding how competitor brands or your own brand vulnerabilities are perceived by activist groups and the general public can inform customer positioning strategy and identify gaps between brand promise and customer values.

Nabeel Azeez, co-owner of Dubai-based email marketing agency Dropkick Copy, argues that open rates are a vanity metric and that revenue is the only metric that matters for ecommerce email programs. His agency segments subscriber lists into distinct groups based on engagement, purchase behavior, and customer value (whales, potential whales, loyal customers, churned whales), then sends targeted emails with messaging customized to each segment—sometimes eight emails per day to different segments for a single client. For brands looking to double email revenue from $10,000 to $20,000 monthly, he recommends increasing send frequency from three times weekly to daily, reviewing automation workflows for missed opportunities, and testing different opt-in incentives (discounts, free products, free shipping, bundles) rather than defaulting to discounts that train customers to expect them.

Strategic hookUnderstanding how to segment customers by purchase behavior and engagement patterns—and tailoring messaging and frequency to each segment—is essential for developing a customer strategy that maximizes lifetime value rather than optimizing for vanity metrics.

Despite token prices collapsing by over 95% in three years—with GPT-4-level quality falling from $30 to under $0.50 per million tokens and DeepSeek V4-Flash now costing $0.03 versus Claude Fable 5's $3.15 for the same task—enterprise AI spending has more than doubled from $3.5 billion to $8.4 billion in six months. This paradox mirrors historical tech patterns: cheaper inputs drive consumption up faster than prices fall. Google processed 3.2 quadrillion tokens monthly by mid-2026, a 330x jump in two years, despite cost-per-token plummeting 60x. The fundamental issue is that AI introduces variable per-call costs that erode margins: AI-native products see 50-60% gross margins versus traditional SaaS's 80-90%, because every inference call burns tokens. Companies are solving this through four cost-control measures—routing to smallest capable models (saving 40-85%), aggressive caching (90% cheaper for repeated input), hard agent caps, and context trimming—then passing variable costs to customers via usage-based pricing models like Salesforce Agentforce (per action), Intercom's Fin ($0.99 per resolution), and GitHub Copilot (usage-based credits).

Strategic hookUnderstanding the shift from fixed seat-based pricing to variable usage-based pricing is essential for building go-to-market strategy and business cases in AI products, as margin sustainability now depends directly on pricing model design rather than unit economics alone.

ENDS TONIGHT? HOW FALSE URGENCY BECAME EMAIL'S $500 PER SUBSCRIBER MISTAKE (6 MINUTE READ)

hook

A Washington State court ruling in April 2025 determined that the Commercial Electronic Mail Act prohibits any false or misleading information in email subject lines, not just deceptive sender identification, opening the door to class action litigation.

A Washington State court ruling in April 2025 determined that the Commercial Electronic Mail Act prohibits any false or misleading information in email subject lines, not just deceptive sender identification, opening the door to class action litigation. The law carries statutory damages of $500 per email per recipient with no cap, leading to nearly 200 lawsuits against major retailers including Old Navy, Nike, Tommy Bahama, Macy's, and Costco over subject lines falsely claiming time-limited sales that were actually extended or recurring. Costco settled for $14 million in June 2026, establishing a benchmark for pending cases, while the legislature reduced damages to $100 per email effective June 2026—but only prospectively, leaving all pre-existing cases under the higher standard. Email is uniquely vulnerable because it creates permanent, searchable records of misleading claims, unlike television or in-store promotions, making it the "forensic surface of marketing" and enabling perfect class action conditions. Companies nationwide face exposure because CEMA applies to any sender with reason to know a Washington resident received the email, federal CAN-SPAM compliance offers no defense, and archived campaigns remain discoverable years after sending.

Strategic hookCompanies should audit whether their email subject line urgency claims—discount validity, deadline enforcement, offer matching—accurately reflect operational reality, as this gap between promise and delivery directly determines customer trust and legal liability.
