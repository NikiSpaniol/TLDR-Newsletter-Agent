DON'T USE QUESTIONS IN YOUR TITLES (1 MINUTE READ)

Research from Stanford University analyzed over 22,000 news articles, 3 million academic articles, and 53,000 Reddit posts, finding that titles using questions instead of statements significantly underperform.

Research from Stanford University analyzed over 22,000 news articles, 3 million academic articles, and 53,000 Reddit posts, finding that titles using questions instead of statements significantly underperform. Question-formatted titles received 71.6% fewer comments, 53.7% fewer upvotes, 12.6% fewer clicks, and 4.9% fewer academic citations across three experiments. The mechanism is that audiences seek clear, certain information when deciding what to read, and questions signal uncertainty, making content appear less informative and valuable. The effect holds regardless of question type (direct, rhetorical, or open-ended) and is likely stronger in uncertainty-avoidant cultures. The recommendation is to replace question titles with assertive statement formats, exemplified by changing Zapier's "App comparisons" collection from question-based titles to formats like "X compared to Y."

The situation is unusual because core CPI inflation hit a post-pandemic low in August, yet UBS expects the Fed to raise rates while lowering long-term inflation projections—something that has never happened before. A CNN analysis notes that rate hikes may cool most investments while leaving AI spending unchecked, potentially allowing the bubble to continue inflating.
