THE EXPENSIVE-LOOP PROBLEM (22 MINUTE READ)

Current large language models excel at "cheap-loop" tasks where feedback is quick, deterministic, and inexpensive—like coding and mathematical problems.

Current large language models excel at "cheap-loop" tasks where feedback is quick, deterministic, and inexpensive—like coding and mathematical problems. However, they perform poorly at "expensive-loop" tasks requiring slow or costly feedback, which comprises most high-value human work in science, business, medicine, and policy. The article identifies four capabilities models lack in expensive-loop domains: intelligent proxy selection (choosing measurable stand-ins for hard-to-measure goals while detecting when proxies fail), theory building (developing causal models to generalize across experiments and prioritize which experiments provide the most information), resource awareness (strategically budgeting limited compute and time rather than wasting resources through repeated trials), and error correction (questioning premises and methods, not just parameters). Models trained in cheap-loop environments develop harmful habits like reward-hacking and confirmation bias that transfer poorly to expensive-loop work, and they rarely generalize from low-cost to high-cost feedback loops the way humans do. Training models to master expensive-loop tasks would require either substantial investment in direct expensive-loop training (which could dominate AI spending) or building "wargame" simulation environments, though critical real-world elements like human interaction and wet-lab experiments resist simulation.

WHEN DO WE NEED ON-POLICY DISTILLATION? DISTILLING ON OFFLINE STUDENT ROLLOUTS IS OFTEN BETTER (1 MINUTE READ)

NVIDIA researchers investigated whether on-policy distillation (OPD), a standard technique for transferring knowledge from large teacher language models to smaller student models, is always necessary.

NVIDIA researchers investigated whether on-policy distillation (OPD), a standard technique for transferring knowledge from large teacher language models to smaller student models, is always necessary. They introduced Semi-OPD, an alternative approach that distills from offline rollouts generated by the initial student model instead. Testing across 17 teacher-student pairs ranging from 1.5B to 235B parameters, Semi-OPD outperformed OPD in 14 cases, achieving up to 13.6% higher accuracy and 11.4x faster training. The effectiveness of each approach depends on the alignment between teacher and student models, measured by output-token overlap ratio: OPD benefits highly aligned pairs, while Semi-OPD provides more stability for misaligned pairs by avoiding off-policy drift as context length increases. The findings suggest that effective distillation requires considering on-policyness with respect to both student and teacher, challenging the assumption that OPD is universally superior.

DECISION MODELS CUT AGENT LLM CALLS BY 29.6% (1 MINUTE READ)

A researcher tested offloading bounded decisions from a main LLM agent to a smaller decision model (Jev) on 360 standard operating procedure cases across referral abuse detection, email intent classification, and patient intake. Using two strategies—checking step completion after tool calls and delegating bounded business decisions with defined allowed outcomes—the approach reduced main LLM calls by 29.6%, API costs by 26.6%, and mean latency by 19.7%, while maintaining near-perfect accuracy (360/360 tasks correct versus 357/360 for the LLM baseline). The key architectural pattern involves supplying evidence to the decision model, constraining choices to allowed options, validating confidence thresholds at 0.8 probability, and falling back to the main LLM when uncertain.
