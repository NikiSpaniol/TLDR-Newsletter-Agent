CitizenLab researchers discovered a new spyware called "AdFection" developed by Israeli firm Discover-Tech that infects devices through online advertising with zero user interaction required, marking the first confirmed case of government-backed zero-click spyware delivered via ads. The malware was deployed across 442 million ads on over 120,000 websites including the BBC and New York Times, targeting journalists at Al Jazeera and officials at NATO, the European Commission, NVIDIA, and Arm. Local newsrooms in the US have shrunk dramatically, with only 7.8 journalists per 100,000 residents in 2026 compared to 40 per 100,000 in 2002, creating an environment where overworked journalists are vastly outnumbered by PR professionals. Successful pitching to local outlets requires understanding their distinct structures: broadcast newsrooms revolve around assignment editors who set the daily agenda, while non-broadcast outlets operate through reporters and editors. The pitch strategy must match the outlet type and format—print needs ready-to-run stories with data, TV needs visuals and on-camera guests, and radio needs articulate speakers—and should include all information upfront to minimize back-and-forth with time-strapped journalists. Building an effective media list involves mapping core local outlets, sizing the newsroom by checking staff pages, and identifying journalists by researching their recent coverage using site-specific searches. Stories succeed locally when they have genuine community impact, feature local people or faces, provide strong visual elements for TV, and offer a timely hook that answers "why now?" The key to success is thinking like a producer: make journalists' jobs easier by targeting the right person, providing localized angles, and timing pitches before morning planning meetings while avoiding periods when breaking news dominates coverage.

Strategic hookUnderstanding how understaffed local newsrooms operate and what information they need upfront directly informs how to design customer outreach and messaging strategy for PR and media relations campaigns targeting local markets.

YOUR NEWSLETTER ISN'T THE UNSUBSCRIBE PROBLEM (3 MINUTE READ)

hook

A Gmail sender's "Needs work" status in Postmaster Tools for unsubscribe compliance typically isn't caused by a single newsletter platform but rather by multiple email systems sending from the same domain without synchronized suppression lists.

A Gmail sender's "Needs work" status in Postmaster Tools for unsubscribe compliance typically isn't caused by a single newsletter platform but rather by multiple email systems sending from the same domain without synchronized suppression lists. When a subscriber unsubscribes from one system like Beehiiv, Gmail measures compliance at the domain level across all senders, so if HubSpot or another tool continues sending from that domain without knowing about the unsubscribe, Google records a compliance violation. The author explains that Google can't access individual platform suppression lists—it only observes whether mail stops after an unsubscribe—and measures this per domain, meaning one sloppy system degrades the reputation of all mail from that domain. The solution involves three steps: syncing unsubscribes across all systems, separating mail streams with distinct From addresses and List-IDs, and ensuring every system has proper one-click unsubscribe headers. Failing to fix this cascade effect means frustrated users hit spam instead of unsubscribe a second time, further damaging domain reputation and potentially affecting the newsletter's inbox placement.

Strategic hookUnderstanding how multiple email systems interact across a single domain is critical for developing a customer engagement strategy that maintains sender reputation and ensures subscribers can actually opt out, directly impacting email deliverability and customer experience.

A STANDING HOLDOUT IS THE ONLY AUDIT AN ALGORITHM CANNOT ARGUE WITH (7 MINUTE READ)

hook

In 2017-2021, major companies including Uber, JPMorgan Chase, Procter & Gamble, and Airbnb discovered that cutting large portions of their digital advertising spend—ranging from 66% to 99%—produced little to no measurable impact on customer acquisition or business outcomes. Uber found $100 million of its $150 million annual performance marketing budget was ineffective, Chase cut 400,000 placements down to 5,000 with no cost impact, P&G eliminated $200 million in digital spend without reducing growth rates, and Airbnb maintained 95% of pre-pandemic traffic with minimal marketing during lockdown. Strategic hookUnderstanding the true effectiveness of marketing channels through holdout testing is critical when building business cases for budget allocation, as dashboards alone cannot reveal whether spending is creating actual incremental business value or merely capturing customers who would have converted anyway.
