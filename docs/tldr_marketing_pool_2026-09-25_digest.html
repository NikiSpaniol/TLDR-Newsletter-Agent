OpenAI's advertising system uses a cookie called `obi` to track ChatGPT users across third-party websites that run OpenAI's ad measurement pixel, creating a cross-site identifier linked to user accounts. The mechanism works by generating a signed token on ChatGPT.com that becomes a cookie scoped to OpenAI's domain, then persists across advertiser sites that load OpenAI's pixel code, collecting browsing behavior including product searches, articles read, and purchase activity. The researcher verified this tracking across 936 distinct advertiser pixels on 1,029 hostnames and found that the cookie is configured with `SameSite=None` specifically to enable cross-site transmission, unlike OpenAI's other tracking cookies. OpenAI classifies `obi` as an analytics cookie under its stated analytics consent rather than marketing consent, yet the mechanism functions identically to Meta's cross-site conversion tracking and creates unprecedented concerns given that ChatGPT users share sensitive information they would not post on social networks. OpenAI did not answer questions about this classification when contacted by the researcher.

Strategic hookUnderstand which of four distinct habit-loop failures is driving usage churn in order to design targeted retention interventions rather than broad pricing responses, which requires segmenting churned users by their engagement curves and conducting qualitative research to connect quantitative patterns to root causes.
