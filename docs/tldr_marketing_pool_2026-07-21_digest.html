WHEN AI CHATBOTS BOOST VS HURT SALES (3 MINUTE READ)

hook

Research from IÉSEG School of Management and University of Lille examined when AI chatbots help or hurt sales across four experiments involving 1,339 participants.

Research from IÉSEG School of Management and University of Lille examined when AI chatbots help or hurt sales across four experiments involving 1,339 participants. The study found that chatbots boost purchase intention by 44.7% for products that are hard to evaluate (like clothing with multiple fit and style considerations), but reduce purchase intent for easy-to-evaluate products with small assortments (like a vitamin line with few options). For easy-to-evaluate products with large assortments (10+ options), chatbots increase engagement and purchase intent by helping customers navigate choice overload. The mechanism works because chatbots help customers manage ambiguity and uncertainty by structuring information, while conversational interaction mimics human interaction and increases engagement. The research was limited to a few product categories and did not account for chatbot design variations, recommendation accuracy, or implementation environment differences.

Strategic hookA company considering chatbot implementation should first conduct customer research and product analysis to determine whether their catalog exhibits high evaluation complexity or choice overload, which determines whether a chatbot investment will meaningfully increase conversion versus waste resources.

+39% CONVERSIONS BY REMOVING A PROMINENT CALL-TO-ACTION (2 MINUTE READ)

hook

A global academic and professional editing service increased conversions by 39% by removing a prominent "Get Quote" call-to-action button from its homepage header.

A global academic and professional editing service increased conversions by 39% by removing a prominent "Get Quote" call-to-action button from its homepage header. Research showed that visitors were clicking the button and jumping directly to a quote form that asked detailed questions (document type, service level, word count, turnaround time) they weren't yet prepared to answer, causing them to abandon the flow. By removing the button, visitors were forced to engage with the rest of the page, where they encountered service descriptions, team credentials, and trust signals that built confidence before encountering quote CTAs positioned on individual service pages. This demonstrates that a prominent early CTA can actually create friction by pushing users toward conversion before they have the knowledge and confidence needed to make an informed decision. The test validated what the firm calls the Safe Step Principle: visitors are more likely to continue when the next step feels appropriate to what they already know.

Strategic hookThis case illustrates the importance of customer-journey mapping and understanding where prospects are in their decision-making process—testing whether aggressive early conversion attempts may actually prevent sales by skipping necessary education and trust-building phases that different customer segments require.
