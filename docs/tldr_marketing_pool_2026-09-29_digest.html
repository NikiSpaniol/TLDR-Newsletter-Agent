HOW GOOGLE AND UBER MEASURE WHAT CANNOT BE RANDOMIZED (5 MINUTE READ)

Google and Uber have industrialized measurement methods for marketing interventions that cannot be randomized or geographically held out, such as national launches, rebrands, and price changes that affect entire populations simultaneously.

Google and Uber have industrialized measurement methods for marketing interventions that cannot be randomized or geographically held out, such as national launches, rebrands, and price changes that affect entire populations simultaneously. The foundational approach is synthetic control, which builds a weighted blend of untreated comparison units to estimate what would have happened without the intervention, then measures the gap between actual outcomes and this counterfactual. Google published CausalImpact, a Bayesian time-series version of this method, while Uber operationalized it for marketplace constraints by treating entire cities as units and alternating treatment and control over time when city-level holdouts fail. These methods are weaker than randomization and vulnerable to manipulation, so their integrity depends on four non-mathematical disciplines: pre-registration of the analysis plan before launch, sufficient pre-period data to validate the counterfactual, placebo testing to confirm the method isn't measuring noise, and honest reporting of credible intervals rather than false point estimates. The article argues that unmeasurable spend should be labeled as such rather than laundered through analyses that simulate rigor, and that mature organizations should reserve these quasi-experimental methods as instruments of last resort, not defaults.

CLIPPING 101: INSIDE THE CLIPPING ECONOMY (7 MINUTE READ)

The clipping economy is a performance-based distribution model where brands pay clipping agencies to coordinate thousands of independent social media accounts to repost short clips of brand content, paying only for posts that exceed a view threshold typically between one thousand and ten thousand views.

The clipping economy is a performance-based distribution model where brands pay clipping agencies to coordinate thousands of independent social media accounts to repost short clips of brand content, paying only for posts that exceed a view threshold typically between one thousand and ten thousand views. Brands provide brief requirements and budgets ranging from thousands to tens of thousands of dollars, while clippers—mostly hobbyists like high school students, college students, and parents with day jobs running existing fan pages—download footage, edit clips, and post to their own accounts, with top earners making up to sixty thousand dollars per month. The model works primarily for entertainment brands like record labels, movie studios, and streamers with deep content catalogs, because it provides both distributed reach across pages the brand doesn't own and a low-cost creative testing ground where the best-performing variations get repurposed as paid ads. However, the ecosystem operates in legal gray areas around copyright infringement, undisclosed advertisements, and view manipulation, and extending the model beyond entertainment to consumer product brands has proven difficult since clippers don't show their faces and therefore cannot demonstrate products effectively.

HOW X'S RANKING ALGORITHM WORKS (3 MINUTE READ)

X published its production "For You" feed ranking algorithm on GitHub in September 2026, updating it every four weeks, providing rare transparency into how posts are ranked.

X published its production "For You" feed ranking algorithm on GitHub in September 2026, updating it every four weeks, providing rare transparency into how posts are ranked. The system uses a three-stage process: retrieval narrows roughly 10 million posts to 2,800 using embeddings and follow relationships, then a transformer model (Phoenix) predicts user actions across 25 engagement types, and finally a weighted sum scores each post. The revealed weights show copy-link shares are worth 20x a like, replies and quotes worth 5x, while mutes and reports carry severe penalties of −58.8 and −234 respectively. Much folk wisdom about X virality proves inaccurate—replies aren't worth 13.5x likes but 5x, and there's no detectable Premium multiplier or boost for author replies in the code. The practical implications are that posts should prioritize shareability and conversation-starting over engagement-baiting, stay topically focused to reach non-followers, and understand that repeated posts from the same author diminish in value and all posts expire from ranking after 48 hours.
