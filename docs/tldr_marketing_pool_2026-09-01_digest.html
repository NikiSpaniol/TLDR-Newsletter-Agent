HOW GEN Z KILLED MILLENNIAL MINIMALISM (6 MINUTE READ)

hook

Millennial brands built their identity around minimalist, Instagram-friendly packaging with clean fonts, muted colors, and ample negative space, establishing a formula that dominated for years through brands like Glossier and Warby Parker.

Millennial brands built their identity around minimalist, Instagram-friendly packaging with clean fonts, muted colors, and ample negative space, establishing a formula that dominated for years through brands like Glossier and Warby Parker. Gen Z, having grown up saturated in this aesthetic, is now rejecting it in favor of brighter, weirder, more illustrative, and personality-driven packaging across categories from beverages to skincare. The shift encompasses five major moves: making "healthy" products more desirable rather than austere (OLIPOP), embracing maximalist illustration and detail (Fishwife), adopting intentionally imperfect or handmade aesthetics (Coconut Cult), creating counter-worthy design objects (Graza), and reframing emotional associations with categories like acne care (Starface). Rather than a single Gen Z style, the movement represents a rebellion against sameness and a pivot toward packaging that expresses distinct brand personality. The author argues this isn't merely a design trend but a fundamental shift reflecting how Gen Z customers define what feels interesting and authentic.

Strategic hookUnderstanding generational differences in aesthetic preferences and emotional drivers—evident in how packaging design directly influences perceived product desirability and category repositioning—should inform customer research and segmentation strategies when developing brands targeting Gen Z audiences.

HOW MUCH OF THE INTERNET IS WRITTEN WITH AI? (5 MINUTE READ)

Pew Research Center analyzed nearly half a million English-language webpages from 2021-2026 using AI detection software to measure how much internet content is AI-authored.

Pew Research Center analyzed nearly half a million English-language webpages from 2021-2026 using AI detection software to measure how much internet content is AI-authored. As of January 2026, approximately one in ten .com domain pages showed signs of AI authorship, compared to 4.6% of .org pages and roughly 1% of .edu and .gov pages, representing a dramatic increase since ChatGPT's November 2022 release. The research identified distinctive linguistic patterns in AI-generated text, including overuse of em dashes (which doubled since 2023), Oxford commas (up 63%), and favored vocabulary words like "delve," "interplay," and "testament" (more than doubled). These AI tells have proliferated across the web as more AI-generated content appears online, with certain words and phrases now appearing at much higher rates than in human-written text.

hook

TextNow conducted a 26-week geo-fenced experiment with a six-week post-treatment window to measure whether YouTube awareness campaigns (optimized for reach and video completion) could drive lower-funnel lift in Total Week 2 Primary Users, a key business KPI. The experiment showed 1.84% total W2PU lift with a cost per incremental user of 98 (indexed against a planning assumption of 100), but critically revealed that 87% of the measured effect accumulated after the first two months, with early readings hovering near zero. This timing insight was essential because short-duration tests of awareness media would have missed or understated the true impact. The results gave TextNow a company-specific benchmark for interpreting future brand campaigns and allowed the growth team to manage internal expectations about when upper-funnel investments would produce measurable lower-funnel outcomes.

Strategic hookUnderstanding the time-lag between awareness media exposure and lower-funnel conversion is essential for accurately evaluating customer journey touchpoints and allocating budget fairly across channels that operate on different payback horizons.

HOW TO BUILD AN INEVITABLE BRAND IN 2026: A GUIDE TO MODERN BRAND STRATEGY (7 MINUTE READ)

hook

Zlatina Petrova, founder of branding firm Brandly, outlines a five-step framework for building "inevitable brands"—those whose time has come and feel obvious in retrospect.

Zlatina Petrova, founder of branding firm Brandly, outlines a five-step framework for building "inevitable brands"—those whose time has come and feel obvious in retrospect. The approach prioritizes discovering a brand's inherent truth from within the organization rather than externally, identifying which of three levels (functional, experiential, or transformational) the brand belongs to, and building emotional memory through "brand frequency" rather than relying solely on repetition. Petrova distinguishes between brand promise (the cognitive, functional claim) and slogans (the emotional articulation), and introduces a brand-positioning matrix using price perception and tradition-versus-modernity axes to guide visual identity decisions. The framework culminates in hiring a design partner who can translate emotional positioning into visual elements, directing them through systematic documentation, and evangelizing the system internally so teams understand the "why" behind brand rules.

Strategic hookCustomer strategy teams can apply the three-level brand positioning framework (functional, experiential, transformational) to map where customer needs and competitive opportunities exist, helping leadership determine whether to compete on product features, experience delivery, or customer identity transformation.

SHALLOW INTENT: WHY PEOPLE DON'T LIKE AI CONTENT, AND HOW TO MAKE IT GREAT (16 MINUTE READ)

Author Tomás Pueyo analyzes why audiences dislike AI-generated content by examining poster submissions to his Uncharted Territories publication, arguing that the core issue is "shallow intent"—AI creates images with visual density that mimics aesthetic sophistication but lacks the intentionality that comes from human creators thinking critically about every element.

Author Tomás Pueyo analyzes why audiences dislike AI-generated content by examining poster submissions to his Uncharted Territories publication, arguing that the core issue is "shallow intent"—AI creates images with visual density that mimics aesthetic sophistication but lacks the intentionality that comes from human creators thinking critically about every element. He identifies "AI grime" (irregular pseudo-textures and ghost edges) as a telltale sign of this shallow intent, and notes that while AI excels at generating quick creative concepts, the execution phase requires human effort to add meaningful depth; he observes the same phenomenon in AI text and AI startups, where the technology's ease of execution becomes an excuse to skip the iterative work that imbues genuine intention into a finished product. The article then reviews actual poster submissions, highlighting examples where promising aesthetics collapse under scrutiny because the underlying logic and connections between elements lack rigor—such as graphs that are inaccurate or layers that don't make scientific sense—ultimately demonstrating that audiences can sense when visual promise isn't backed by substantive thought.

Reddit's citation share in ChatGPT dropped dramatically from 3.8% to 0.5% between mid-August, representing an 86% decline according to tracking platform Promptwatch. The collapse began around August 8 with a steeper fall on August 14, though OpenAI stated it doesn't set fixed visibility levels for individual sites and that ChatGPT still cites Reddit. Ahrefs publishes every article with AI assistance but maintains quality by applying rigorous human judgment at each stage. The company defines AI slop as content published without sufficient human understanding, evidence, or original contribution, arguing the problem lies not in AI use itself but in abdicating editorial responsibility. Their approach involves upstream human work before drafting (establishing reader, promise, point of view, and evidence needs), creating decision gates between research, outline, and draft stages where humans must pass judgment, and reinvesting time savings into richer content features like tools and data visualizations rather than just volume. HELPFUL CONTENT GRADER GPT (17 MINUTE READ)

hook

Steve Toth built a GPT and Claude AI tool that automatically grades web pages against Google's 32-question "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" self-assessment guide, published in December 2025.

Steve Toth built a GPT and Claude AI tool that automatically grades web pages against Google's 32-question "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" self-assessment guide, published in December 2025. The grader scores each question as PASS, FAIL, PARTIAL, or N/A, then rolls the results into a letter grade (A–F) across four sections: Content & Quality, Expertise, People-first, and Avoid Search-Engine-First content. It offers three weighting modes—equal, section-weighted (default), and YMYL-adaptive (for health, finance, legal, or safety topics)—to reflect how much each bucket matters for rankings. The tool provides one-line evidence for every verdict tied to actual page text, plus a prioritized list of the 3–5 fixes that would most improve the score. Toth built this because manually checking pages against all 32 questions was slow, inconsistent, and required regrading when team members disagreed.

Strategic hookThis tool directly supports customer research and content-strategy development by enabling teams to audit whether their content aligns with explicit, published Google ranking signals—a concrete way to validate whether pages meet identified customer information needs and search intent before publishing.

THE SHARED AGENT BRIEF: A PRACTICAL OPERATING AGREEMENT FOR GTM TEAMS (23 MINUTE READ)

hook

The article describes a framework called the "Shared Agent Brief" for aligning cross-functional GTM teams when using AI agents.

The article describes a framework called the "Shared Agent Brief" for aligning cross-functional GTM teams when using AI agents. The author, drawing from experience building AI workflows at S2O with colleagues managing sales, product, and AI engineering, identifies a core problem: connecting tools and providing AI with context doesn't solve the underlying issue of conflicting instructions from different team members. The solution involves five components—shared objective, role inputs, decision rights, AI boundaries, and decision records—that clarify what each person contributes (evidence, recommendations, decisions, approvals), whose authority governs each decision, and where AI must stop. The framework is tested through two account scenarios: one where compatible inputs allow the AI to safely complete internal preparation, and one where conflicting relationship information requires the AI to escalate to the relevant human decision-owner before proceeding. The author provides practical prompts and worksheets for teams to select their first workflow, interview stakeholders, map decision rights, and implement a weekly review cycle.

Strategic hookOrganizations implementing multi-functional AI-driven GTM processes need to conduct customer/internal stakeholder interviews to establish clear decision-rights maps and role boundaries, ensuring that customer insights from sales conversations inform but don't automatically override product, marketing, or AI workflow decisions.

Unfold, a design agency founded by Eddie Lobanovskiy, shares insights from working with enterprise clients including Facebook, Ripple, Merck, Solana, Rumble, and Lamar. The core lesson is that while the actual design process remains constant across company sizes, everything surrounding it changes dramatically—enterprise work requires navigating strict onboarding (NDAs, background checks, procurement systems), multiple stakeholder layers with competing feedback, and approval timelines measured in months rather than days. The article emphasizes that success depends less on creative genius and more on understanding enterprise decision-making structures, designing for two audiences simultaneously (end users and internal executives), maintaining reliability, and learning to extract the actual concern beneath contradictory feedback from numerous stakeholders.

hook

Walmart's advertising business grew 43% in the second quarter of 2026, with the retailer generating $6.4 billion in ad revenue, up 46% year-over-year, though this represents only 1% of total revenue. However, retail media operates at approximately 70% profit margins compared to Walmart's core retail business at around 4%, making it far more lucrative; Amazon dominates the U.S. retail media market with $76 billion in annual ad revenue, while Target reported $915 million in advertising revenue last year, more than doubling from $522 million two years prior. The article argues that retailers and brands are mislabeling retail media as "brand building" when it is actually a bottom-of-the-funnel, defensive investment that reaches only shoppers already in-store and ready to purchase, not consumers building mental availability. The author emphasizes that while brands cannot ignore retail media given major retailers' market dominance, they should recognize it as an evolved form of slotting fees and treat it as a trade promotion destination, not brand-building strategy.

Strategic hookMarketers need to conduct honest channel-attribution analysis to understand whether retail media budgets are truly incremental or cannibalizing more strategic brand-building spend that drives long-term growth.
